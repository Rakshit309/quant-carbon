## The problem with Black-Scholes for carbon

Black-Scholes assumes prices follow Geometric Brownian Motion:
dS = r·S·dt + σ·S·dW

Under GBM, prices drift upward on average, volatility is proportional
to price, and there is no equilibrium anchor. This is a reasonable
approximation for equity indices.

Carbon prices have a structural anchor. The EU ETS regulatory cap
creates a policy equilibrium — the price where the marginal cost of
abatement equals the permit price. When EUA prices deviate far from
this level, market forces pull them back.

## The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck solution
dS = κ(θ − S)dt + σ·dW

The `κ(θ − S)dt` term is the restoring force. When S > θ, this term
is negative — pulling the price down. When S < θ, it is positive —
pulling the price up.

## Calibrating from data

The OU process in discrete time is an AR(1) regression:
S(t+dt) = α + β·S(t) + ε

From 48 months of EU ETS data (2021–2024):

| Parameter | Value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| κ (kappa) | 2.26/year | Strong mean reversion |
| θ (theta) | €72.50/tonne | Long-run equilibrium |
| σ (sigma) | €24.81/√year | Absolute volatility |
| Half-life | 3.7 months | Deviations halve in 3.7 months |
| R² | 78% | Model explains 78% of price variation |

## The positive theta insight

For an ATM EUA call with S=65, K=65, θ=60, κ=1.0, T=1:

The OU forward price is:
F = S·e^{−κT} + θ·(1−e^{−κT})
= 65·0.368 + 60·0.632
= 23.9 + 37.9 = 61.84

The forward price (61.84) is BELOW the strike (65). As time passes,
the forward price rises back toward spot. The option becomes more
at-the-money. Value increases. **Theta is positive.**

Black-Scholes always produces negative theta. This is not a bug —
it is a fundamental limitation of the GBM assumption applied to
mean-reverting processes.
