## Why carbon credits need ECL provisioning

As carbon accounting standards mature, companies holding voluntary
carbon credit portfolios will need balance sheet provisions against
permanence risk — the risk that the underlying carbon removal is
reversed before the permanence period ends.

A reforestation project burns down. A methane capture facility shuts.
A government reverses land-use policy. The credit has been retired
against a net-zero commitment, but the physical abatement no longer exists.

This is a credit risk problem. The IFRS 9 ECL framework applies directly.

## The framework
ECL = PFP × LFF × EAD
where:
PFP  = Permanence Failure Probability  (≡ PD)
LFF  = Loss Factor on Failure          (≡ LGD)
EAD  = Tonnes × price per tonne        (≡ EAD)

## Stage assignment

| Stage | Condition | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Low permanence risk | 12-month ECL |
| Stage 2 | SICR triggered | Lifetime ECL |
| Stage 3 | Confirmed reversal | Full write-off (EAD) |

SICR triggers:
- Country risk score > 0.6
- Physical climate risk > 0.7
- Unverified registry
- Annual PFP doubles from base rate

## Lifetime PFP

Survival analysis over the remaining permanence period:
P(failure before T) = 1 − (1 − annual_pfp)^T

This is the same cumulative PD curve used in IFRS 9 lifetime ECL.

## Worked example

A 10,000 tonne REDD+ portfolio at €15/tonne:
EAD          = 10,000 × €15 = €150,000
Annual PFP   = 3.5% base × country adjustment × climate adjustment
× registry discount × seasoning discount
≈ 4.13%
LFF          = 65% (partial recovery through jurisdictional buffers)
12m ECL      = 4.13% × 65% × €150,000 = €4,028
Coverage     = €4,028 / €150,000 = 2.69%

The CFO provisions €4,028 against a €150,000 carbon credit portfolio.

## Scenario-weighted ECL

IFRS 9 requires probability-weighted ECL across multiple scenarios.
Pass `scenarios: true` to receive base/upside/downside ECL:

```json
{
  "scenarioWeighted": [{
    "base":     4028.39,
    "upside":   2841.17,
    "downside": 6892.44,
    "weighted": 4447.62
  }]
}
```
