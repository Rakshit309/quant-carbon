## Why two models

Most quantitative tools apply Black-Scholes to carbon options.
Black-Scholes assumes prices follow Geometric Brownian Motion —
they drift upward on average with no pull toward any equilibrium.

Carbon prices do not behave this way.

<CodeGroup>
```
Equity — Geometric Brownian Motion
dS = r·S·dt + σ·S·dW
```
Carbon — Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
dS = κ(θ − S)dt + σ·dW
</CodeGroup>

The OU process has three parameters:
- **κ** — mean-reversion speed (how fast price returns to equilibrium)
- **θ** — long-run equilibrium price (policy anchor, e.g. €72/tonne for EU ETS)
- **σ** — absolute volatility in €/tonne per √year

Calibrated from 48 months of EU ETS data, EUA prices show a
**half-life of 3.7 months** — deviations from equilibrium halve
in under four months.

## What is built

| Module | Contents |
|---|---|
| `src/core/` | BS, Monte Carlo GBM, Crank-Nicolson FDM |
| `src/models/carbon/` | OU process, Bachelier pricing, OLS calibration |
| `src/models/carbon/permanenceRisk.ts` | IFRS 9 ECL for carbon credits |
| `src/models/risk/portfolioVar.ts` | Monte Carlo VaR + Expected Shortfall |
| `src/api/` | Hono REST API on Cloudflare Workers |
